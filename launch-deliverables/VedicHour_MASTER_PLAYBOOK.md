*The best of both worlds: the Claude/Opus plan **and** GPT's plan — independently audited, scored, fact-checked against the real product, and rebuilt into one foolproof, copy-paste, AI-generates-everything manual.*

**Built for a 20-year-old who will only copy-paste.** Every step names the exact tool + URL, gives the exact prompt to paste (text, image, **and** video generation), and says where the output goes. Your owned tools (Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude) are used first; external spend is kept within ~$200 and only where it clearly wins.

## How to use this
1. Read **Audit & Scoring** to see what was taken from each plan and why.
2. Work top to bottom: Positioning → Offer → Tools/Drive → Design → Video → Organic → Paid → SEO/GEO → Outreach → Lifecycle → Calendar.
3. Run the **SQL at the very end** (one paste) to switch on attribution + referrals.
4. Generate everything with AI using the exact prompts; never write or design by hand.

# Audit & Scoring


## Audit A — Claude/Opus plan (scored)

This is a self-audit of my own deliverables: `launch-deliverables/MARKETING_PLAYBOOK.md` (the "how") and `launch-deliverables/MARKETING_CONTENT_PACK.md` (the "what" — 30-day calendar, 15 Reel scripts, 15 image prompts, 10 carousels, 12 Reddit/Quora answers, 10 FB posts, 5 cold emails, full paid-ad copy, 15 Pinterest pins, an X launch thread + 20 tweets, 8 YouTube titles, 15 email subject lines). I graded it the way a paid principal marketing auditor would grade a vendor deliverable handed to a 20-year-old beginner with ~$200 to spend. No grade inflation: this plan was written by me, and I am the harshest reviewer it will get.

**Overall score: 71 / 100** — a genuinely strong *content library* bolted onto a *weak operating system*. The raw writing is publication-grade and the positioning is correct. But it asks a copy-paste beginner to juggle ~18 tools, mixes organic and paid copy with real ad-policy risk, hand-waves the hardest channels ("setup pattern is identical everywhere"), and sets no honest revenue number. It is a 90/100 content pack stapled to a 55/100 execution manual.

### What's strong

- **The positioning is genuinely best-in-class and ruthlessly consistent.** Every single asset — captions, scripts, ads, pins, tweets — returns to the same wedge: "most apps give one horoscope for 24 hours; we rate all 18 planetary hours of *your* day, Swiss-Ephemeris precise, AI plain-English." That is the correct category to own and the discipline of repeating it is the plan's biggest asset. Even the GPT plan's own audit conceded "the strongest idea was correct."
- **The content volume and quality are the real deliverable.** 30 paste-ready captions with hooks + 15 hashtags each, 15 fully-storyboarded Reel scripts (hook + on-screen beats + voiceover + CTA + audio style), 12 value-first Reddit/Quora answers that would actually pass a moderator, 5 personalized cold emails, complete Google + Meta ad copy, 15 Pinterest pins with SEO descriptions, an X launch thread. A beginner could post for two months without writing a word. This is the part of the plan worth keeping almost verbatim.
- **The "AI makes everything, you only paste" framing is the right operating philosophy** for a 20-year-old with no design skill, and the weekly "30 min → 7 posts" assembly line is a real, repeatable ritual rather than vague advice.
- **Measurement instinct is correct.** It mandates UTM on every link, points to the already-built /admin Acquisition tab, and prescribes a monthly "kill what doesn't pay, double down on what does" review. It also correctly *uses* the existing infrastructure (admin dashboard, lifecycle emails) instead of re-proposing it.
- **The Reddit/Quora answers are unusually disciplined** — they lead with genuine teaching, bury the product mention in the last line, and explicitly tell the founder to drop the link entirely in no-promo subs. That is the only way that channel works and most playbooks get it wrong.
- **Compliance instinct is present in the organic voice:** "no fear," "teacher not punishment," "astrology won't predict your lottery numbers," "decide for yourself." The tone is calm and credible, which protects the brand.

### What's weak

- **No revenue target, no funnel math — the single biggest flaw.** The plan never says what success is. A copy-paste beginner needs "here is the number, here is roughly how many free Kundlis → paid reports that implies, here is what $200 of ads realistically returns." Without it, the founder can't tell a good week from a bad one, and the silence quietly implies the channels will work better than they will. (Score 3/10 on Realism is the honest mark.)
- **Tool overload.** The plan names ~18 tools. It pays lip service to "start with Instagram + YouTube only" but then documents Canva, Buffer, Meta Suite, Linktree, Dub, Apollo, Gemini, ChatGPT image, Veo, Sora, GSC, Bing, GBP, plus six social platforms — and routes ad clicks through Linktree-adjacent thinking. A beginner reads the whole document and freezes. The GPT plan's critique here is fair and lands.
- **Paid and organic copy are not separated, and several lines are ad-policy landmines.** "Astrology apps lie to you with rounded math," "Don't sign anything in the next 90 min," "the best and worst hours of your day," "the sky is on your side" — these are fine-ish as educational organic posts but will draw Meta/Google rejections or trust penalties as *paid* creative, because they imply the viewer faces personal jeopardy. The plan reuses the same voice for both, which is the exact mistake that gets ad accounts flagged.
- **The hardest channels are hand-waved.** Part 2 literally says "Full step-by-step setup… is in this file's earlier structure" and "the setup pattern is identical everywhere" for Facebook, TikTok, Pinterest, X, Reddit, and Quora. It is *not* identical (Reddit karma gates, TikTok's algorithm, Pinterest's SEO mechanics all differ), and "earlier structure" points at content that isn't actually there. This is the plan breaking its own foolproofness promise at the moment it matters most.
- **Video production is under-specified.** The scripts are excellent but the manufacturing step is a single paragraph. There is no named, click-by-click Canva (or CapCut) flow, no guidance on which trending audio or how to find it, and no use of the AI video tools the founder could lean on (HeyGen avatar, ElevenLabs voice, Veo/Sora B-roll) beyond a one-line mention. For a founder who "creates nothing by hand," the bottleneck is exactly here.
- **Paid-ads method (not copy) is thin.** No campaign structure (CBO vs ABO), no realistic budget pacing beyond "$5–10/day," no audience-size sanity check, no creative-testing matrix, and no definition of "a winning ad" before telling the founder to scale. Pointing a beginner at Ads Manager with good copy but no method burns the $200.
- **GEO is the missed open goal.** This product's natural moat is being *cited by AI engines* (ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity) when people ask "best Vedic astrology app" — and there's already a blog post that ranks VedicHour #1. The plan covers classic SEO (GSC/Bing) but does almost nothing to engineer AI-citation, which is the highest-leverage, lowest-cost channel available to this exact founder.
- **Coupon risk acknowledged elsewhere but not flagged here.** The plan plasters `NEWUSER30` on nearly every asset. That's fine *because the code is real and works* — but the plan never tells the founder to verify it resolves at checkout before mass-posting. (It does, per canonical facts, so this is low-risk — but the plan should say "confirm it works once, then post.")

### What to keep

- **The entire Content Pack, near-verbatim.** The 30-day calendar, 15 Reel scripts, 15 image prompts, 10 carousels, 12 Reddit/Quora answers, 5 cold emails, Pinterest pins, X thread/tweets, YouTube titles, and email subject lines are the strongest part of either plan. Keep them as the master content library.
- **The "AI makes everything → you paste/click/schedule" philosophy and the weekly 30-min assembly line.** This is the correct operating model for this founder.
- **The positioning, tagline, and the "rate all 18 horas" wedge** — unchanged. Both plans agree, and it's right.
- **The UTM-everything → /admin Acquisition → monthly kill/scale review** measurement loop. Keep and harden it with actual targets.
- **The Apollo cold-email motion and the revshare + trackable-coupon hook** for partners. Keep the 5 variants; add reply-handling.
- **The organic compliance voice** (calm, "teacher not punishment," "decide for yourself").

### What to drop (or fix)

- **Drop the all-tools-at-once sprawl.** Cut the active stack to: Gemini (images/video) + ChatGPT/Claude (words, already done) + one scheduler (Meta Business Suite, free) + Instagram + YouTube Shorts. Everything else moves to an explicit "Phase 2, only after 20 posts are a habit" appendix. One owned external purchase max (Canva Pro ~$13/mo) and only if it demonstrably speeds the founder up.
- **Drop the reused-voice-for-ads approach.** Split copy into two banks: organic (educational, topical, current voice) and paid (calm, never implies the viewer's personal hardship, no "lies/don't sign/worst hours"). Rewrite the flagged paid lines.
- **Drop the "setup is identical everywhere" hand-wave.** Either write the real button-by-button steps for each Phase-2 channel or remove the channel from the plan. No pointers to "earlier structure" that doesn't exist.
- **Fix the goal vacuum.** Add one honest base target (align with the GPT plan's ~$2,000 launch-week figure as a stretch, not a base, given $200 spend + zero existing audience), a simple funnel (visits → free Kundli → paid report at a stated conversion assumption), and "what $200 of ads actually buys."
- **Fix video production.** Add one named, click-by-click Reel workflow and a short "if you want a talking-head, use HeyGen + ElevenLabs with this prompt" path.
- **Add a real GEO play** (get cited by ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity) — currently the biggest unforced omission.

**Bottom line:** I'd ship the Content Pack tomorrow and rebuild the Playbook. The library is an asset; the operating manual over-promises foolproofness, under-specifies the two hardest jobs (paid + video production), separates nothing for ad-safety, and refuses to name a number. Strong raw material, weak system — 71/100.

### Dimension-by-dimension scoring

#### 1. Strategic positioning & differentiation — 6 / 10

**Strength.** The GPT plan correctly identifies the strategic crown jewel: VedicHour should own *hour-by-hour Vedic timing*, not generic astrology. Its Section 1.1 names this as "the strongest idea" and Section 2 builds the whole document around "clearer and heavier timing windows." It also adopts the ad-safe line "Not another horoscope. A personal Vedic timing grid." verbatim and correctly — that line matches our canonical facts exactly. Reframing the product as a "timing dashboard / Vedic timing grid" rather than "AI astrologer" (Section 2.4) is genuinely good positioning advice: it raises perceived trust and dodges the low-rent "talk to astrologer" category.

**Error.** The differentiator is stated *softly*. The canonical, defensible wedge is concrete and numeric: **VedicHour rates all 18 planetary hours (horas) of your day, computed with Swiss Ephemeris + Lahiri ayanamsa.** The GPT plan almost never says "18 hours," never says "Swiss Ephemeris" in its positioning or hooks, and never says "Lahiri ayanamsa." Instead it uses the vaguer "timing windows," which is true but blunts the proof. "We rate all 18 of your hours with astronomer-grade math" is a sharper, more screenshot-able claim than "clearer and heavier windows," and it is the single fact no competitor app can copy. Under-using it costs the plan a point or two of differentiation.

**Fix to reach 8+:** Hard-wire "all 18 planetary hours" and "Swiss Ephemeris + Lahiri ayanamsa" into the core hooks and the one-line explanation. Keep "clearer/heavier windows" as the compliance-safe softener, but lead with the number.

#### 2. Product-fact accuracy — 5 / 10

This is the dimension where the GPT plan does the most damage, because errors here get printed onto ads, bios, and video end-cards where they are expensive to unwind.

**Strength.** It is admirably disciplined about the coupon: Section 3.4 says "use a coupon only if it actually exists" and gates `NEWUSER30` behind verification. That instinct is exactly right for a beginner. (In our case the verification resolves to *yes* — `NEWUSER30` is real and safe to advertise — but the cautious framing is good hygiene.) It also correctly lists the real product ladder names (Free Kundli, Deep Kundali, Kundli Matching, 7-day/Monthly/Annual) in Section 3.2.

**Errors — these must be corrected before anything ships:**

1. **It changed the tagline.** The GPT plan's main tagline (Section 2.1, repeated in the bio Section 6.3, the master prompt Section 10, and dozens of scripts) is **"Your Kundli, decoded hour by hour."** The **real, live tagline is "Your Life, Decoded Hour by Hour."** This is not interchangeable. "Your Life" is broader, more emotional, and is what's already on the site, in the lifecycle emails, and in the brand. Shipping "Your Kundli…" would fork the brand voice across channels on day one. **Every instance of "Your Kundli, decoded hour by hour" must be globally replaced with "Your Life, Decoded Hour by Hour."**

2. **No prices anywhere.** The plan's revenue mix (Section 3.3) invents its own price points — "Annual Oracle 25 × ~$1,250" implies ~$50 (close), but "Deep Kundali 40 × ~$400" implies ~$10 and "Kundli Matching 25 × ~$250" implies ~$10 — and never states the actual prices. The real numbers are: 7-Day $9.99, Monthly $19.99, Annual $49.99, Deep Kundli $9.99 / ₹899, Gun Milan $9.99 / ₹899. The plan should *quote* these, not imply them, and should note the AED/INR geo-pricing via Ziina.

3. **Linktree is treated as the bio link (Sections 5/22 setup checklist).** This isn't wrong as a tactic, but it ignores that **every link must carry UTM params** so the /admin Acquisition tab attributes it — the plan only adds UTMs in the partner/affiliate section (Part 2, §18), not on the primary social links. (See Dimension 8.)

4. **Page coverage is thin.** It names vedichour.com and gestures at "free Kundli," but never routes to the specific high-intent URLs that already exist and convert: `/free-kundli`, `/kundali` (Deep Kundli), `/synastry` (matchmaking), `/pricing`. A beginner following this plan would send all traffic to the homepage and lose the intent-matched landing pages.

**Fix to reach 9+:** Global find-replace the tagline; insert a real price table with the canonical USD/INR figures and a one-line Ziina geo-pricing note; route each CTA to its exact page; confirm `NEWUSER30` as verified-real and stop hedging it.

#### 3. Channel strategy & prioritization — 8 / 10

**Strength.** This is one of the plan's best dimensions, and its **focus discipline is its signature virtue.** Section 1.2 explicitly calls out the previous plan for "too many tools" and "too broad" a content set, then collapses everything to *three pillars → three formats → two paid channels → one contact tracker* (Section 1.3). It refuses to chase every platform (Section 19.1 ranks them: Reels → Shorts → TikTok → LinkedIn → WhatsApp), and its "Final Decision Rules" (Section 26) — "do not add more tools," "repeat the winning hook," "do not chase every platform" — are exactly the rules a 20-year-old beginner needs to avoid drowning. The two-paid-channel choice (Google Search for intent + Meta for discovery) is correct for this product.

**Gap.** It treats **SEO/content as essentially absent** from the channel mix (it appears only as a vague afterthought), even though VedicHour already has a 17-post blog (including a "best Vedic astrology platforms 2026" comparison that ranks VedicHour #1) and 44 programmatic SEO pages live. For a $200-budget launch, organic search + GEO (getting cited by AI answer engines) is the highest-ROI channel available and it's already built — so omitting it from the channel strategy is a real prioritization miss. (Scored further in Dimension 4.)

**Fix to reach 10:** Add "owned SEO/blog/GEO" as a first-class channel — specifically, submit the sitemap, and repurpose the existing 17 blog posts into Reels/Shorts scripts (the content already exists; it just needs reformatting, which is free).

#### 4. Use of existing assets — 3 / 10

**This is the plan's biggest blind spot and its lowest score.** The GPT plan was written as if VedicHour were a bare domain with nothing built. In reality, the founder is sitting on infrastructure that most launches would pay thousands to have, and the plan neither knows about it nor uses it.

**What it misses, specifically:**

1. **The /admin analytics dashboard already exists** — with Acquisition (reads `utm_source` on every visit), Revenue, Retention, a Call-list CRM (uses captured phone numbers), and Ops tabs. The GPT plan instead tells the founder to **rebuild all of this by hand in Google Sheets** (Sections 5, 23, and the entire Part 3 tracker templates: Contact Tracker, Content Tracker, Ad Tracker). That's dozens of hours of manual data entry re-creating a dashboard that already auto-populates. The Sheets are not useless (the contact-status tracking has value the dashboard doesn't), but the *performance/attribution* tracking is pure duplication.

2. **Lifecycle emails are already LIVE via Resend** (domain verified): report-ready, welcome-on-signup, abandoned-checkout recovery, and a founder daily digest. The GPT plan writes a **4-email sequence from scratch** (Section 21) and a separate free-user follow-up sequence (Part 2 §12) — re-proposing email flows that already exist and are sending. The founder would be building a parallel, manual email system on top of an automated one.

3. **WhatsApp via Twilio is already wired** (founder just needs to add `TWILIO_*` keys to activate). The GPT plan treats WhatsApp purely as manual one-by-one DMs (Sections 19, 25; Part 2 throughout) and never mentions that automated WhatsApp is one config step away.

4. **The 17-post SEO blog + 44 programmatic SEO pages + the #1-ranking comparison post** are not leveraged at all. This is a built GEO/SEO engine — the plan ignores it.

5. **Brand assets already exist** at `/brand/logo-square.svg`, `/brand/social-bg-square.svg`, `/brand/social-bg-story.svg`. The GPT plan instead tells the founder to **generate a fresh logo and backgrounds in Gemini** (Section 7). Generating new ones risks brand drift from the real, shipped assets. (Generating *additional* variations is fine; replacing the canonical ones is not.)

**Why this matters:** every one of these is something the plan asks the founder to *build*, that is *already built*. For a beginner with limited time, "use the dashboard you already have" beats "spend a weekend reconstructing it in Sheets."

**Fix to reach 8+:** Rewrite the tooling/tracking sections to (a) point to /admin instead of hand-built Sheets for performance + revenue + retention; (b) reference the live Resend lifecycle emails and only add what's missing; (c) note the one-step WhatsApp/Twilio activation; (d) treat the existing brand SVGs as canonical; (e) add the blog/SEO engine as a content source and GEO asset.

#### 5. AI-generates-everything (incl. video GENERATION) — 5 / 10

**Strength.** For text and still images, the plan is genuinely AI-first and gives paste-ready prompts: a strong master content prompt (Section 10), an outreach-personalization prompt (Part 2 §15), logo/banner/background image prompts (Section 7), and a tidy 60-minute daily workflow (Section 25). The image prompts are well-constrained (navy #0A0A1A, gold #D4AF37, "no gods, no temples, no faces") which keeps output on-brand and safe.

**Errors / gaps:**

1. **No video-GENERATION prompts.** This is the standout miss against our standard. The plan's video SOP (Section 12) is "make a still background in Gemini → add text + voice in CapCut." That's manual assembly, not AI generation. It never gives a single ready-to-paste prompt for **Sora, Google Veo (via Gemini), Runway, Kling, or Pika** to *generate motion video*, nor a **HeyGen** avatar-video prompt, nor an **ElevenLabs** voiceover script/voice-direction block you can paste. A true AI-generates-everything plan hands the founder a copy-paste Veo/Sora prompt that yields a finished cosmic clip.

**Fix to reach 9:** Add a "Video generation" block with paste-ready prompts for Google Veo (via the Gemini app the founder already owns — zero extra cost), Sora (ChatGPT Plus — already owned), and an optional HeyGen avatar script + ElevenLabs voice-direction prompt, each stating exactly where the output file goes next (download → drop into the post/ad). Lead with the already-owned Gemini/ChatGPT video tools before any paid one.

#### 6. Foolproofness for a copy-paste beginner — 8 / 10

#### 7. Compliance & brand safety — 10 / 10

**This is the plan's single best dimension and its most reusable contribution.** It deserves to be lifted wholesale into the master playbook.

**Strength.** The compliance framework is comprehensive and correct:
- It **separates paid-ad copy from organic copy** (Section 16, Section 24) — paid must be safer and must never imply the viewer's personal hardship. This is precisely the regulator/ad-policy distinction most beginner plans miss.
- It gives an explicit **safe-language / avoid-language vocabulary** (Section 24): use "clearer/heavier timing windows," never "guaranteed," "bad luck," "fix your life," "avoid disaster," "save your marriage," "cure," "predict exactly."
- It bans **sensitive targeting** (religion, relationship hardship, negative financial status, health, trauma) in Section 16.3 — a real ad-account-survival rule.
- It provides a ready **disclaimer**: "For reflection and planning only. Not medical, legal, financial, or emergency advice." (Section 6.4 / 24).
- It rewrites risky lines into safe ones ("Are you struggling in marriage?" → "Explore Vedic compatibility themes privately.") in Section 24.

This framework is materially safer than what my own content pack ships with. **Note for the master playbook:** my content pack contains a few lines that violate exactly these rules and must be scrubbed using GPT's framework — e.g., "Don't sign anything in the next 90 min," "Astrology apps lie to you with rounded math," "best and worst hours," and "when the sky is actually on your side." GPT's plan would (correctly) flag and soften all of these.

**No deduction.** The only nuance: the framework is built around the (slightly off) "timing windows" language rather than the sharper "18 horas," but that's a positioning point (Dimension 1), not a safety one.

#### 8. Measurement & attribution — 6 / 10

**Strength.** The plan is metrics-literate: it defines a performance dashboard with good-by-Day-7 targets and danger signs (Section 23), explicit kill rules and scale rules (Sections 23.1–23.2), and per-channel trackers (Part 3). It *does* introduce UTM links — correctly formatted — for partner/affiliate attribution (Part 2 §18: `?utm_source=partner&utm_medium=creator&utm_campaign=launch_week&utm_content=PARTNERNAME`).

**Errors:**

### 5.2 FACELESS video SOP — button-by-button in CapCut (your default workflow)

> **Goal:** turn one script (Section 5.1) into a finished 9:16 MP4 with cosmic background, captions, voiceover, and an end card — in ~12 minutes, with zero filming and zero design skill. CapCut is free. Install the **desktop app** from `https://www.capcut.com/tools/desktop-video-editor` (the desktop version exports without a watermark; the mobile app is fine too but check the watermark toggle on export).

**Step 0 — Gather your 2 ingredients first (do these before opening CapCut):**
1. **The background clip or image.** Either generate a 5–8s cosmic video (Section 5.3 — best) or use a still PNG (Section 5.3 has the image prompt too). Save it to a folder `04 CapCut Videos`.
2. **The voiceover MP3.** Generate it in ElevenLabs (Section 5.5 — best) or you'll use CapCut's built-in text-to-speech in Step 6. If using ElevenLabs, save the MP3 to the same folder.

**Step 1 — New project.** Open CapCut → click **Create project** (or **New project**) on the home screen.

**Step 2 — Set the canvas to vertical.** Bottom-right, find the **Ratio** button (looks like a rectangle/aspect icon). Click it → choose **9:16**. The preview goes tall. This is the format for Reels, Shorts, and TikTok.

**Step 3 — Import your background.** Top-left panel → **Media** → **Import** → select your cosmic clip/PNG from `04 CapCut Videos`. It appears as a thumbnail. **Drag it down onto the timeline** (the strip at the bottom).

**Step 4 — Fit the background to full screen.** Click the clip in the timeline so it's selected (white border). In the preview, drag a corner handle outward until the cosmic background fills the whole tall frame (no black bars). If it's a short clip and your video needs to be longer, right-click the clip → **Copy**, then **Paste**, and drag the copy to follow it — or grab the clip's right edge and stretch it (for images, you can stretch freely).

**Step 5 — Add the voiceover (if you made one in ElevenLabs).** **Media → Import** your MP3 → drag it onto the timeline **below** the video clip (audio sits on its own track). It auto-aligns to the start. Skip to Step 7.

**Step 6 — OR generate the voiceover inside CapCut (free fallback).** Left panel → **Text** → **Add text** → type your full VO line. With that text selected, open the right panel → **Text-to-speech** → pick a voice (choose a calm one — try "Lori", "Chips", or any clear English voice; avoid robotic ones). Click **Apply**. CapCut creates an audio clip from the text. (You can delete the on-screen text afterward if you only wanted the audio, or keep it as a caption.)

**Step 7 — Auto-generate captions (the big on-screen words).** Left panel → **Captions** (or **Text → Auto captions**) → **Auto captions** → it listens to your voiceover and writes synced captions automatically. Click **Generate**. Then style them: select the captions track → right panel → **Style** → set font to a bold clean sans (CapCut's "Arial Bold" or any heavy font), **color white**, add a subtle **gold (#D4AF37) outline or shadow** to match the brand, and bump the size so it's readable on a phone. Drag the caption block to the lower-center of the frame.

> If auto-captions misread a word (e.g. "hora" or "nakshatra"), double-click that caption and fix the spelling. Always proofread — wrong captions kill trust.

**Step 8 — Add your hook as a big title (0–2s).** Left panel → **Text → Add text** → type the **Hook** line from the script. Make it the biggest text on screen, white with a gold accent. In the timeline, trim this text clip so it only shows for the first ~2 seconds. Add a gentle entrance: select it → right panel → **Animation → In →** pick **Fade** or **Rise**.

**Step 9 — Add the end card (last 3 seconds).** **Text → Add text** at the very end of the timeline. Type two lines:
```
Try your free Kundli
VedicHour.com
```
Center it, white + gold. Optionally drop your logo on top: **Media → Import** `public/brand/logo-square.svg` (export it as PNG first if CapCut won't take SVG — open the SVG in your browser, screenshot it, or ask Gemini to "render this as a 1080x1080 transparent PNG"). Place the logo small, centered above the text.

**Step 10 — Add trending audio at low volume (optional but boosts reach).** On **mobile CapCut**: **Audio → Sounds →** browse trending → pick a calm/cinematic track → add it. Lower its volume to ~15–20% so it sits under the voiceover (select the music clip → **Volume** slider down). On desktop, you can also just add the music in Instagram/TikTok *after* uploading (the apps reward using their native trending audio — see Section 5.6).

**Step 11 — Export.** Top-right → **Export**. Settings: **Resolution 1080p**, **Frame rate 30**, format **MP4**. Filename like `vedichour_script01_18hours.mp4`. Save to `04 CapCut Videos`. **Check there's no CapCut watermark** — if you see one, on mobile toggle off "Add CapCut watermark" before exporting; on desktop the default export is clean.

**Step 12 — Done.** You now have one master MP4. Repeat for 3 scripts a day. Then jump to Section 5.6 to push the one file to all four platforms.

> **Speed tip:** make a CapCut **template** out of your first finished video. Keep the canvas, caption style, end card, and logo; just swap the background clip, the voiceover, and re-run auto-captions for the next script. After the first one, each video takes ~7 minutes.

### 6.7 Daily engagement tactics (30–45 min/day — this is where reach is won)

The algorithm rewards *conversation*, not just posting. A post with 20 thoughtful comment-replies in the first hour will out-travel an identical post you ignored. Daily routine:

#### The first-60-minutes protocol (per post)

1. **Reply to every comment** within the first hour, especially with a question back ("What's your Moon sign? I'll tell you your nakshatra trait" — this is literally built into several calendar posts). Replies = comments = ranking signal.
2. **Pin your best comment** containing the free-kundli UTM link (use the IG Reel UTM link). Link-in-comment is how you get a clickable link on a Reel without sending people to the bio.
3. **Heart + reply to the first ~10 comments** to seed momentum.

#### Daily outbound engagement (the "give-to-get" loop)

- **Comment genuinely on 10–15 posts/day** from adjacent accounts: bigger Vedic astrology creators, nakshatra/dasha pages, spirituality accounts, wedding/matchmaking pages. Add real value (answer a question, add a fact) — not "great post 🔥". This puts @vedichour in front of *their* audience and often earns a profile visit. This doubles as soft partner-warming for the outreach motion.
- **Answer Stories questions** in your niche; reshare relevant ones with a thoughtful add.
- **Use the calendar's interactive Stories** (polls: "Mars hora vs Venus hora," question stickers: "Drop your Moon sign") — interaction stickers massively boost Story reach and feed DMs you can convert.

#### DM handling (warm leads land here)

- When someone DMs asking about their chart, reply helpfully with one genuine insight, then: *"You can pull your full free Kundli here 👉 [IG Story UTM link] — and code NEWUSER30 takes 30% off the deeper reports if you want them."* Helpful first, link second.
- Save good exchanges as anonymized testimonials (ask permission before reposting).

#### Comment-to-content loop

- Every "great question" in your comments is your next Reel. Screenshot it, answer it on camera/text. This is the cheapest, highest-performing content source — real audience questions out-perform invented hooks.

#### Cross-pollination

- Repost your best-performing Reel as a Story the next day with a "ICYMI" sticker + link sticker.
- Drop your strongest carousel into relevant **Facebook groups** (caption + value, link only if the group allows it — the content pack section 6 has 10 group-safe posts written for this).

#### What NOT to do (account-safety)

- Don't follow/unfollow in bulk, don't comment the identical line on 50 posts, don't buy followers — these get accounts throttled or banned and the GPT plan flags them explicitly.

### 11.7 Kill / Scale Decision Rules

**KILL a creative or ad if ANY of these is true:**
1. It gets clicks (Acquisition shows visits) but **zero free-Kundli leads** after 24h.

3. Watch time is low / comments are confused ("what is this?").
4. It reads fear-based, exaggerated, or implies the viewer's hardship — kill on sight, it's an ad-policy risk.
5. It pulls only freebie-hunters and no buyers after Day 4.

> To kill: in Meta/Google Ads set the ad's status to Off. In your Content Tracker mark it `KILL`. Do not delete — keep it as a record of what didn't work.

**SCALE (raise budget / make more variants) only if ALL of these are true:**
1. It produces **free-Kundli leads** (Acquisition).
2. It produces **at least one purchase or checkout start** (Revenue).
3. It's policy-safe (no fear, no guarantees, no personal-hardship targeting).
4. It matches the positioning ("Your Life, Decoded Hour by Hour" / "not another horoscope").
5. Its **cost per lead is under ~$2** (for paid).

> To scale: raise that one ad's daily budget by ~50-100% (e.g. $10 → $20), and make 3-5 new variations of its hook. **Scale the winner, never the average.** Never raise the whole campaign budget — only the proven ad inside it.

**The $200 budget guardrail:** start ads at $10/day each (Meta + Google). Only increase spend onto an ad that has already produced a purchase. If by Day 4 **no** ad has produced a purchase, cut paid ads to $5/day and put your energy into organic + outreach (which cost $0 and, at this scale, usually drive more of the revenue anyway). Never let total ad spend cross ~$150 in launch week unless you are clearly profitable on a channel.

  ON public.user_profiles (referral_code) WHERE referral_code IS NOT NULL;
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS public.referrals (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  referrer_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  referee_id  UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,

ALTER TABLE public.referrals ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY;
