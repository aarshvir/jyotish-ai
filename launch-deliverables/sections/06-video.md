### 5.2 FACELESS video SOP — button-by-button in CapCut (your default workflow)

> **Goal:** turn one script (Section 5.1) into a finished 9:16 MP4 with cosmic background, captions, voiceover, and an end card — in ~12 minutes, with zero filming and zero design skill. CapCut is free. Install the **desktop app** from `https://www.capcut.com/tools/desktop-video-editor` (the desktop version exports without a watermark; the mobile app is fine too but check the watermark toggle on export).

**Step 0 — Gather your 2 ingredients first (do these before opening CapCut):**
1. **The background clip or image.** Either generate a 5–8s cosmic video (Section 5.3 — best) or use a still PNG (Section 5.3 has the image prompt too). Save it to a folder `04 CapCut Videos`.
2. **The voiceover MP3.** Generate it in ElevenLabs (Section 5.5 — best) or you'll use CapCut's built-in text-to-speech in Step 6. If using ElevenLabs, save the MP3 to the same folder.

**Step 1 — New project.** Open CapCut → click **Create project** (or **New project**) on the home screen.

**Step 2 — Set the canvas to vertical.** Bottom-right, find the **Ratio** button (looks like a rectangle/aspect icon). Click it → choose **9:16**. The preview goes tall. This is the format for Reels, Shorts, and TikTok.

**Step 3 — Import your background.** Top-left panel → **Media** → **Import** → select your cosmic clip/PNG from `04 CapCut Videos`. It appears as a thumbnail. **Drag it down onto the timeline** (the strip at the bottom).

**Step 4 — Fit the background to full screen.** Click the clip in the timeline so it's selected (white border). In the preview, drag a corner handle outward until the cosmic background fills the whole tall frame (no black bars). If it's a short clip and your video needs to be longer, right-click the clip → **Copy**, then **Paste**, and drag the copy to follow it — or grab the clip's right edge and stretch it (for images, you can stretch freely).

**Step 5 — Add the voiceover (if you made one in ElevenLabs).** **Media → Import** your MP3 → drag it onto the timeline **below** the video clip (audio sits on its own track). It auto-aligns to the start. Skip to Step 7.

**Step 6 — OR generate the voiceover inside CapCut (free fallback).** Left panel → **Text** → **Add text** → type your full VO line. With that text selected, open the right panel → **Text-to-speech** → pick a voice (choose a calm one — try "Lori", "Chips", or any clear English voice; avoid robotic ones). Click **Apply**. CapCut creates an audio clip from the text. (You can delete the on-screen text afterward if you only wanted the audio, or keep it as a caption.)

**Step 7 — Auto-generate captions (the big on-screen words).** Left panel → **Captions** (or **Text → Auto captions**) → **Auto captions** → it listens to your voiceover and writes synced captions automatically. Click **Generate**. Then style them: select the captions track → right panel → **Style** → set font to a bold clean sans (CapCut's "Arial Bold" or any heavy font), **color white**, add a subtle **gold (#D4AF37) outline or shadow** to match the brand, and bump the size so it's readable on a phone. Drag the caption block to the lower-center of the frame.

> If auto-captions misread a word (e.g. "hora" or "nakshatra"), double-click that caption and fix the spelling. Always proofread — wrong captions kill trust.

**Step 8 — Add your hook as a big title (0–2s).** Left panel → **Text → Add text** → type the **Hook** line from the script. Make it the biggest text on screen, white with a gold accent. In the timeline, trim this text clip so it only shows for the first ~2 seconds. Add a gentle entrance: select it → right panel → **Animation → In →** pick **Fade** or **Rise**.

**Step 9 — Add the end card (last 3 seconds).** **Text → Add text** at the very end of the timeline. Type two lines:
```
Try your free Kundli
VedicHour.com
```
Center it, white + gold. Optionally drop your logo on top: **Media → Import** `public/brand/logo-square.svg` (export it as PNG first if CapCut won't take SVG — open the SVG in your browser, screenshot it, or ask Gemini to "render this as a 1080x1080 transparent PNG"). Place the logo small, centered above the text.

**Step 10 — Add trending audio at low volume (optional but boosts reach).** On **mobile CapCut**: **Audio → Sounds →** browse trending → pick a calm/cinematic track → add it. Lower its volume to ~15–20% so it sits under the voiceover (select the music clip → **Volume** slider down). On desktop, you can also just add the music in Instagram/TikTok *after* uploading (the apps reward using their native trending audio — see Section 5.6).

**Step 11 — Export.** Top-right → **Export**. Settings: **Resolution 1080p**, **Frame rate 30**, format **MP4**. Filename like `vedichour_script01_18hours.mp4`. Save to `04 CapCut Videos`. **Check there's no CapCut watermark** — if you see one, on mobile toggle off "Add CapCut watermark" before exporting; on desktop the default export is clean.

**Step 12 — Done.** You now have one master MP4. Repeat for 3 scripts a day. Then jump to Section 5.6 to push the one file to all four platforms.

> **Speed tip:** make a CapCut **template** out of your first finished video. Keep the canvas, caption style, end card, and logo; just swap the background clip, the voiceover, and re-run auto-captions for the next script. After the first one, each video takes ~7 minutes.

### 5.5 ElevenLabs voiceover — prompt, settings, and workflow

> **Why:** CapCut's built-in text-to-speech is fine, but ElevenLabs sounds dramatically more human and premium — and premium voice is half the perceived quality of an astrology video. The **free tier (~10,000 characters/month)** is enough for roughly 15–20 short scripts. If you want more, **Starter is ~$5/month** — easily within budget. Optional but recommended.

**Setup — button-by-button:**
1. Go to `https://elevenlabs.io` → **Sign up** (free).
2. Left menu → **Text to Speech** (or **Studio → Text to Speech**).
3. **Pick a voice:** click the voice dropdown → open the **Voice Library** → search and audition. Good picks for this brand (calm, warm, credible, not spooky, not guru-like):
   - For a **neutral/global English** read: try **"Rachel"**, **"Charlotte"**, or **"Daniel"**.
   - For an **Indian-English** read (often best for this audience): search the library for **"Indian"** and audition the calm male/female options; pick one with a warm, unhurried tone.
   - Pick **one signature voice and use it on every video** — consistent voice = sonic branding.
4. **Settings (this is the important part — set these every time):**
   - **Model:** choose the highest-quality multilingual model available (e.g. "Eleven Multilingual v2" or the current flagship).
   - **Stability:** ~**50%** (steady but not flat).
   - **Similarity / Clarity:** ~**75%**.
   - **Style exaggeration:** **low (0–20%)** — we want calm and credible, not dramatic.
   - **Speed:** slightly **slower than default** for a premium, reflective feel.
5. **Paste the VO line** from any script in Section 5.1 into the text box.
6. **Pronunciation fix:** ElevenLabs may stumble on Sanskrit terms. Spell them phonetically in the box (it won't show on screen since this is audio only): `hora` → `hoh-rah`, `nakshatra` → `nuck-shuh-truh`, `dasha` → `duh-shuh`, `Kundli` → `Koond-lee`, `muhurat` → `moo-hoor-tuh`, `Jyotish` → `Jyoh-tish`. Listen back; re-spell until it's right.
7. Click **Generate**, listen, then **Download** the MP3 to `04 CapCut Videos`.
8. Import it into CapCut (Section 5.2 Step 5).

> **One master voice direction to keep in your notes (paste as a reminder when auditioning):** "Calm Indian-English or neutral English, premium, warm, credible, unhurried — like a thoughtful teacher, not a spooky fortune-teller and not a hype salesman."
