## 2. Offer, Pricing & Realistic Goals

This section is the money math. It tells you exactly what you're selling, for how much, what to expect in launch week, and the one coupon rule that keeps you out of trouble. Read it once, then keep the two tables (revenue mix + daily tracker) open in a tab all week.

The hard truth up front, because honest math beats hopeful math: **you have ~$200 of ad budget and zero audience.** A real, hittable launch-week target is **$2,000**. $5,000 is possible but only if one specific thing happens (a video breaks out or a partner pushes traffic) — it is a *bonus*, not a plan. Everything below is built around the $2,000 number.

### 2.2 The launch offer (what you actually announce)

You do **not** invent a fake discount or a fake deadline. You have one real, working coupon and one honest framing. Use exactly these:

**The headline offer — announce this everywhere:**

```text
Start with a free Vedic report — one free, no card needed.
Then, if it's useful, your first paid report is 30% off with code NEWUSER30.
```

**Why this works:** the free preview removes all risk (the customer pays nothing to find out if they like it), and **NEWUSER30 is a real coupon that genuinely works** — it gives 30% off the first paid report. That turns a $9.99 Deep Kundli into **$6.99**, which is an easy impulse "yes" after someone already got value from the free report. You are safe to advertise NEWUSER30 in ads, posts, emails, and DMs because it actually exists in the system.

**The launch-week framing (honest urgency, no fake countdown):**

```text
Launch week is live — NEWUSER30 gets 30% off your first paid report.
```

That's it. No "luck running out," no "last chance before your stars change," no fake 24-hour timer. The discount is the reason to act; the launch is the occasion. (See compliance rule in the master playbook — fear-based urgency is banned.)

### 2.3 The $2,000 base goal — the revenue-mix math

You will not hit $2,000 from one product. A single $9.99 report would need ~200 sales; a single $49.99 Annual would need ~40 cold strangers to trust you with the top-tier product in week one — neither happens with no audience. **You hit it with a mix:** lots of cheap easy "yes"es, a handful of big ones. Here is the honest blend, with the coupon already factored into the realistic per-sale revenue:

| Product | Price | Sales needed | Realistic net per sale* | Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Deep Kundli ($9.99) | $9.99 | **70** | ~$8.00 | **~$560** |
| Kundli Matchmaking ($9.99) | $9.99 | **40** | ~$8.00 | **~$320** |
| 7-Day Forecast ($9.99) | $9.99 | **15** | ~$8.50 | **~$128** |
| Monthly Oracle ($19.99) | $19.99 | **15** | ~$18.00 | **~$270** |
| Annual Oracle ($49.99) | $49.99 | **16** | ~$46.00 | **~$736** |
| **TOTAL** | — | **~156 paid sales** | — | **~$2,014** |

\* "Realistic net per sale" assumes a chunk of buyers use **NEWUSER30** (30% off → first paid report nets less) and a small slice is lost to payment fees. It is deliberately conservative so the plan survives reality.

**Read the table this way:** the cheap reports (Deep Kundli + Matchmaking, 110 sales) carry the *volume* and prove demand; the **16 Annual Oracle sales carry the margin** — they alone are ~37% of revenue. So your two jobs all week are: (1) get a *flood* of $9.99 first purchases from cold traffic + outreach, and (2) convert ~16 warmer, higher-intent people (warm contacts, repeat free-users, partner audiences) up to the Annual.

**Is ~156 sales realistic?** Sanity-check the funnel backward (use these as your daily targets):

```text
~156 paid sales   ← the goal
needs ~300+ free-preview signups   (≈ half of engaged free users buy something, eventually, with follow-up)
needs ~1,000+ clicks to the site   (≈ 30% of clickers start a free report)
needs ~50,000+ organic video views + ~$200 ads + ~300 outreach touches
```

Those click/view/signup numbers match the daily content + outreach targets elsewhere in this playbook (3 videos/day, 20 outreach touches/day, $10/day ads). **The goal is reachable — but only if the daily activity actually happens.** Activity is the input; $2,000 is the output. There is no shortcut.

**Where the sales come from (so you know where to push):**

| Source | Share of the ~156 sales | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| Warm + partner outreach | ~40% | Cheapest, fastest trust. Your contact list is your first sales team. |
| Organic short-form video | ~35% | Free reach; the engine that scales if a video pops. |
| Paid ads ($200) | ~15% | $200 buys ~1,000–2,000 clicks; a primer, not the main engine. |
| Free-user follow-up (email/WhatsApp) | ~10% | Lifecycle emails are already live — they convert free users on autopilot. |

If ads alone were carrying this, $200 could not do it. **Outreach + organic do the heavy lifting; ads just prime the pump.** This is why the master playbook spends so many words on contacts and content, not on ad budget.

### 2.4 The $5,000 stretch — and exactly what must happen for it

$5,000 is **not** the plan. It is what happens *if you get lucky on top of doing the plan.* Do not budget for it, do not promise it to yourself, do not spend more on ads chasing it. It only becomes real if **at least one** of these breakout events occurs:

```text
[ ] One short-form video crosses ~100k+ views (a "breakout") and sends a wave of free signups.
[ ] One partner/creator with a real audience actually posts about VedicHour (not just replies "yes").
[ ] One niche community (a wedding/matchmaking group, an NRI forum) adopts the Matchmaking report.
```

If one of those fires, here's what changes in the math — note you do **not** need more of everything, you need more *volume through the same funnel*:

| Lever | Base ($2,000) | Stretch ($5,000) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Free-preview signups | ~300 | **~700+** |
| Deep Kundli sales | 70 | **~150** |
| Matchmaking sales | 40 | **~90** |
| Monthly Oracle sales | 15 | **~35** |
| Annual Oracle sales | 16 | **~40** |
| Total sales | ~156 | **~370** |
| Total revenue | ~$2,014 | **~$5,050** |

**The honest rule:** *Run the $2,000 plan every single day. If a breakout happens, pour fuel only on the thing that's working* — make 5 more versions of the winning video, send the winning partner more assets, post the winning hook again. **Do not** invent new campaigns chasing $5,000; double down on the one proven winner. Stretch comes from amplifying a winner, never from spreading thinner.

### 2.5 The coupon-safety rule (read this; it protects you)

There are two coupons in the system. One you advertise freely; one you must never type in public.

| Coupon | What it does | Use it? |
|---|---|---|
| **NEWUSER30** | 30% off the first paid report. **Real, works, customer-facing.** | ✅ **Advertise it everywhere** — ads, posts, emails, DMs, partner links. |
| **ADMIN100** | 100% off (owner-only testing tool). | ❌ **Never** put it in any public post, ad, caption, screenshot, or DM. If it leaks, strangers get free paid reports and you lose money. |

**The three non-negotiable coupon rules:**

1. **Only ever advertise NEWUSER30.** It's the only customer coupon, and it genuinely works, so it's 100% safe to promote. Saying "30% off your first report with NEWUSER30" is honest and accurate.
2. **Never mention, screenshot, or type ADMIN100 anywhere a customer could see it** — not in a post, not in a Canva graphic, not in a demo video, not in a DM. Treat it like a password.
3. **Never invent a coupon that doesn't exist.** Do not write "SAVE50," "LAUNCH40," or any code you haven't confirmed is live in **/admin → Coupons**. Advertising a dead code makes the customer hit an error at checkout — that's the fastest way to lose a sale and your credibility. If you want a *partner-specific* code later, create it first in /admin, confirm it works, *then* share it.

> **One-line takeaway:** Promote **NEWUSER30** loudly, guard **ADMIN100** like a password, invent nothing.
